# Initial Idea for Necromancy

*Conceived on July 24, 2026*

The goal of **Necromancy** is to create a gameplay experience that feels fundamentally different from Hytale's traditional classes. I do not want the Necromancer to be simply "a mage with pets," but rather a class with its own identity, centered around soul management, summons, and unique resources. Playing as a Necromancer should require a different mindset than playing a Warrior, Archer, or Mage, offering a fresh experience while remaining intuitive and accessible to any player.

The main difference between the Necromancer and a traditional pet system is that summons exist solely to fulfill a purpose during combat. They do not permanently follow the player or provide passive stat bonuses. Instead, they are tactical tools that must be managed carefully. Summoning creatures consumes mana, and overusing necromantic power can cause **Soul Corruption**, a secondary resource that forces the player to decide when deploying their entire army is worth the risk and when conserving resources is the better choice.

Combat should remain strategic at all times. Summons are meant to assist the player, but they should never be capable of completing encounters on their own. Enemies must react to summoned creatures, attacking and eliminating them before turning their attention back to the player. This forces the player to manage both their available summons and the mana required to bring them back into battle. The intention is to prevent infinite summoning without consequences, ensuring that combat always presents meaningful decisions.

One of the core pillars of the mod is the soul capture and storage system. Defeating an enemy gives the player a chance to obtain its soul, allowing that creature to be summoned in the future. The probability of obtaining a soul depends on several factors, including how much the player contributed to defeating the enemy, with damage dealt by the player's summons also counting toward that contribution. Acquiring multiple copies of the same creature requires defeating it repeatedly, encouraging exploration and farming powerful enemies.

Captured souls are stored in a dedicated inventory with limited capacity. The player can carry more souls than they can summon simultaneously, but not an unlimited amount, requiring careful planning before entering a dungeon or facing a boss. This inventory can be expanded through progression, possibly by defeating necromancy-related bosses or consuming particularly powerful souls.

Mana is designed as a continuously consumed resource rather than a one-time casting cost. Every active summon permanently reserves a portion of the player's available mana while it remains in combat. When a summoned creature dies, the reserved mana is not restored immediately; instead, it slowly regenerates over time, representing the exhaustion of the bond between the Necromancer and their summons. As the player progresses, stronger creatures may require the same percentage of mana as weaker ones, or existing summons may simply become more efficient.

Soul Corruption acts as a second resource that the player must constantly manage. Excessive use of necromancy, storing too many souls, or maintaining too many active summons may gradually increase corruption. Once certain thresholds are reached, penalties begin to appear, such as periodic self-inflicted damage, weakened summons, or other negative effects. This ensures that the player is always balancing two essential resources: mana and the stability of their own soul.

The Necromancer's progression should also be integrated into the game world. The class may not be available from the beginning and instead becomes unlocked only after obtaining the player's first soul. From that point onward, a specialized altar or workstation would allow the player to organize their equipped summons in a way similar to preparing spells. The player would have a limited number of quick-access summon slots, which could only be modified at this station, reinforcing the importance of preparation before each adventure.

The user interface should remain as simple as possible. If feasible, the system should avoid complex menus altogether and instead rely on a radial menu or gesture-based controls for quickly selecting summons. The objective is for the complexity to come from strategic decision-making rather than from the interface itself.

Visually, summoned creatures could be slightly distinguished from their original counterparts to communicate that they have truly been resurrected through necromancy. However, this should be implemented carefully to maximize compatibility with other mods and avoid requiring additional configuration whenever possible.

There are also several optional ideas worth exploring during development. Particularly powerful souls could occasionally rebel against the player after being summoned, even serving as a small easter egg. Likewise, creatures considered holy or inherently pure might refuse to serve a Necromancer altogether and instead become hostile if the player attempts to summon them.

As both a testing tool and an endgame feature, the mod could include a mode that allows the player to summon an exceptionally large number of creatures simultaneously in order to observe the complete system in action and evaluate both gameplay balance and performance.

---

Beyond the mod itself, this project serves as a professional demonstration of combat design. Every piece of documentation will clearly explain what is being built, how it will be built, which tools are required, what the acceptance criteria are, and how every system will be validated through testing. The goal is for both the repository and its documentation to reflect a development process similar to that of a professional game studio, demonstrating skills in both game design and software engineering.
